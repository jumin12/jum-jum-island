Game concept

Working title: Tropical Tides: Merge Resort

You inherit a run-down island resort. Players merge ingredients, drinks, food, decorations, and repair materials to complete guest orders and rebuild different areas of the island.

The main fantasy is:

Merge supplies → prepare orders → serve guests → restore the resort → unlock new stories and generators.

Main merge board

Use a board around 8×10 or 9×11 tiles.

Players tap generators to create level-one items:

Generator	Example merge chain
Fruit Stand	Lime → Orange → Pineapple → Fruit Basket
Drink Station	Cup → Juice → Smoothie → Tropical Cocktail
Ice Chest	Ice Cube → Ice Cup → Ice Bucket → Sculpted Ice
Beach Kitchen	Bread → Sandwich → Lunch Plate → Seafood Feast
Tool Shed	Nail → Hammer → Toolbox → Repair Kit
Gift Shop	Shell → Bracelet → Souvenir → Luxury Gift

Generators consume energy, and higher-level generators create better items.

The expanded order system

Instead of orders simply disappearing when completed, give players several ways to fulfill them.

Standard orders

Guests request one or more merged items.

Example:

Level 5 pineapple drink
Level 3 fruit bowl
Reward: coins, experience, and renovation stars
To-go orders

The hanging order tickets from the screenshot could appear above the main board. Players drag completed drinks or meals from the board into the matching ticket.

These orders could have:

Bonus rewards for quick completion
Several items in sequence
Special VIP customers
Combo bonuses for completing multiple tickets together
Resort tasks

Some items are used for rebuilding rather than serving.

Examples:

Merge tools to repair the beach bar
Merge plants to restore a tropical garden
Merge furniture to renovate guest rooms
Merge diving equipment to reopen the marina

This gives players a reason to build multiple item chains instead of only filling food orders.

A distinctive mechanic: the serving lane

Keep the satisfying drink-serving idea, but integrate it into the board.

At the bottom or side of the board, add a serving lane with three slots. Players place completed items there, and guests collect them in order.

Possible strategy:

Loading three matching orders creates a tip multiplier.
Serving the wrong item blocks a slot temporarily.
VIP orders require the correct garnish or presentation.
Some guests leave special items behind, such as keys, photographs, or mystery boxes.

This would help your game feel different from a standard merge title.

Progression structure

Divide the island into areas:

Beach Juice Shack
Boardwalk Café
Tropical Garden
Surf Shop
Marina
Boutique Hotel
Hidden Lagoon
Sunset Festival Grounds

Each location introduces a new generator, merge chain, character, and gameplay rule.

For example, opening the marina could unlock fishing supplies and seafood recipes, while the hotel unlocks linens, room-service orders, and furniture restoration.

Story direction

A simple story framework could be:

The player returns to an island once owned by their family. A storm has damaged the resort, guests have stopped visiting, and a large company is trying to purchase the property. By restoring each area, the player uncovers family secrets and reconnects with the island community.

Characters could include:

A competitive neighboring resort owner
A retired chef with hidden recipes
A marine biologist protecting the lagoon
A surfer who helps restore the beach
A mysterious guest connected to the resort’s past
Features that expand the genre

To make the game feel deeper without becoming overwhelming:

Recipe discovery: Merge ingredients in unusual combinations to unlock permanent recipes.
Weather changes: Sunny, rainy, or stormy conditions affect guest orders.
Board expansion: Repair sections of the resort to unlock additional board tiles.
Generator upgrades: Let players choose between faster recharge, better drops, or larger storage.
Character abilities: Certain characters can reduce energy costs or increase tips.
Seasonal festivals: Limited-time merge chains feed back into the permanent resort.
Guest reputation: Different guest groups unlock as the resort improves.
Strong MVP

For the first playable version, keep the scope controlled:

One 8×10 merge board
Three generators
Approximately 25–35 items
Three item chains
Ten standard orders
Five to-go orders
One renovation location
One short character storyline
Energy, coins, experience, and storage
A tutorial lasting under five minutes

The main test is whether merging, clearing space, and delivering items feels satisfying. Story, events, and monetization should come after the core board works.
